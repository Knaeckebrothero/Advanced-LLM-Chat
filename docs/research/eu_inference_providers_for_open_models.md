# Keep your open-model chats inside Europe

A privacy-minded hobbyist in Germany can test almost every current open-weight model without sending a prompt to China, Singapore or a US router. The setup is **Scaleway Generative APIs** (Paris) as the primary provider, **Berget AI** (Stockholm) for Kimi, and the Vienna router **Cortecs**, set to EU-only zero-retention mode, for the long tail (MiniMax, Mistral Large 4, Qwen3.8-2.4T). Scaleway is the only large EU cloud that serves the current Chinese frontier models self-serve. It offers DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 at €0.60/€1.80 and GLM-5.3 at €1.80/€5.50 per million input/output tokens, plus Qwen3.8-27B, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Qwen3.5-397B and gpt-oss-120b. It also has zero data retention by default, a Paris data center, a written statement that it is not subject to the US CLOUD Act, 1M free tokens, and documented streaming with parallel tool calls. No big EU cloud hosts Kimi. Kimi K3 costs about €3/€15 at every EU host, and Berget pairs it with the most concrete privacy terms in the notes: no stored prompts, metadata-only logs and Swedish-only sub-processors. The model you called "Kimi K3.1" is **Kimi K3.1 Pro**. It has been live only on Moonshot's own API since 23 October, with weights promised by 4 November, and Berget has already scheduled its K3 endpoint to upgrade to K3.1 Pro on 6 November. Waiting a week is better than using Moonshot's Singapore API, whose terms let Moonshot use your content to improve its services. The biggest risk is in the app, not the provider. LangChain's `ChatOpenAI` explicitly does not keep `reasoning_content`, and Kimi needs that field sent back unchanged during tool loops, so the app needs a small provider adapter before Kimi results can be trusted. On a homelab AMD GPU, the 27-35B tier (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Qwen3.8-27B, Gemma 4, gpt-oss-20b) runs well through llama.cpp or AMD's official vLLM image. Every frontier model needs 400 GB to 1.4 TB of memory, so those stay on APIs.

## Seven model families matter, and Kimi K3.1 Pro has no public weights yet

At the end of October 2026 the open-weight frontier is a group of very large Chinese mixture-of-experts models plus a few Western ones. Moonshot announced **Kimi K3.1 Pro** on 23 October as "the first model in our new K3.1 series and our most capable 3T-class open-weight model to date." It has a 1M-token context, is available on the Kimi API, and Moonshot says the full model weights "will be released by Nov 4" ([Kimi blog](https://www.kimi.ai/blog/kimi-k3-1-pro)). As of 30 October there is no K3.1 repository on Hugging Face ([Hugging Face moonshotai](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai)). Moonshot's model list names `kimi-k3.1-pro` and a faster `kimi-k3.1-pro-highspeed` ([Kimi platform docs](https://platform.kimi.ai/docs/models)). **K3.1 Pro runs in thinking mode only.** Requests that disable thinking return HTTP 400, and a mistyped model ID quietly falls back to "K2.8 Preview" with no thinking ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/moonshot-ai-releases-kimi-k3-1-pro-first-model-in-new-k3-1-series/)). Artificial Analysis (AA) ranks it 4th overall with a score of 55, up from 44 for Kimi K3, but another AA page lists 46 ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/kimi-k3-1-pro-launch-benchmarks)). So it is the strongest open-weight model, by a margin AA's own pages disagree on. Until its weights ship, the newest Kimi an EU host can actually run is **Kimi K3**: 2.8T total and 104B active parameters, a 1,048,576-token context, image input, and thinking that is always on ([GitHub MoonshotAI/Kimi-K3](https://github.com/MoonshotAI/Kimi-K3)).

After Kimi, AA's current open-weights table ties **DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017** and **GLM-5.3** at 32. Next come Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro at 30, MiniMax M3.1 Flash at 29, Mistral Large 4 at 26, Meta's Muse Spark Open at 25 and **Qwen3.8-27B** at 20, the best model under 40B ([Artificial Analysis open-source](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/open-source)). AA recently changed its index method, so the Kimi scores and this table should not be compared directly. "Flash" no longer means small: DeepSeek V4.1 Flash is 552B with 16B active per AA, MiniMax M3.1 Flash is 428B with 23B active ([Codersera](https://codersera.com/blog/minimax-m3-1-flash-complete-guide-2026/amp/)), and GLM-5.3-Flash is 320B ([RunAIHome](https://runaihome.com/blog/glm-5-3-open-weights-live-hardware-guide-2026/)). Mistral released the **Mistral Large 4** weights on 29 October. It has about 1T parameters, takes multimodal input natively, offers three reasoning modes, and costs $0.70/$2.10 on Mistral's API ([Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-large-4-oss/)). OpenAI's **gpt-oss-120b/20b** (August 2025, Apache 2.0) has no successor ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-oss/)). It is still the easiest baseline for this app, because `backend/services/llm_provider.py` already adds gpt-oss's `Reasoning: <level>` line to the start of every system prompt.

Licensing barely matters for a private user calling an API. DeepSeek V4.x and GLM-5.3-Flash are MIT, and Gemma 4, Qwen3.5 and gpt-oss are Apache 2.0 ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemma-4/)). The custom licenses on Kimi K3, GLM-5.3, Mistral Large 4 and Muse Spark Open are aimed at model-as-a-service resellers above $20M revenue or companies above $10B ([RunAIHome](https://runaihome.com/blog/glm-5-3-open-weights-live-hardware-guide-2026/); [Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-large-4-oss/); [Meta](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open)). The one EU-specific trap is Meta's **Llama 4 license, which reportedly excludes EU-domiciled users** ([innFactory](https://innfactory.ai/en/ai-models/meta-llama/)). Meta has ended the Llama line anyway, so there is no reason to test it.

## Scaleway is the only big EU cloud serving DeepSeek and GLM self-serve

Among the established European clouds, only Scaleway runs the current Chinese frontier models as serverless endpoints. Its serverless catalog includes `glm-5.3` (256k context, 16k max output), `deepseek-v4.1-flash` (text and vision, 256k context, 32k output), `deepseek-v4-flash-0731`, `qwen3.8-27b` and `qwen3.6-35b-a3b` (both with vision), `qwen3.5-397b-a17b`, `gemma-4-26b-a4b-it`, `gpt-oss-120b` and `mistral-medium-3.5-128b`. It has **no Kimi model of any kind**, and MiniMax runs only on dedicated deployments ([Scaleway supported models](https://github.com/scaleway/docs-content/blob/main/pages/generative-apis/reference-content/supported-models.mdx)). Prices per million tokens, input/output, are €1.80/€5.50 for GLM-5.3, €0.60/€1.80 for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (cached input €0.12), €0.40/€0.80 for V4 Flash 0731, €0.60/€3.30 for Qwen3.8-27B, €0.25/€1.50 for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and €0.15/€0.60 for gpt-oss-120b. The first 1M tokens are free, and the Batches API costs half ([Scaleway pricing](https://www.scaleway.com/en/pricing/model-as-a-service/)). The Chat Completions endpoint at `https://api.scaleway.ai/v1` supports `stream`, `tools`, `tool_choice` and `response_format` ([Scaleway OpenAI compatibility](https://www.scaleway.com/en/docs/generative-apis/reference-content/openai-compatibility/)). The model docs mark function calling and **parallel tool calling "Yes"** for GLM-5.3, both DeepSeek versions and every Qwen model. GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash take reasoning efforts of `none`, `low`, `high` and `max`. There are two catches. **Scaleway's gpt-oss-120b does not support tool calling through Chat Completions**, and Scaleway says to use the Responses API instead. The docs and the pricing page also disagree on whether the DeepSeek model string ends in `-1017` ([Scaleway supported models](https://github.com/scaleway/docs-content/blob/main/pages/generative-apis/reference-content/supported-models.mdx)).

Scaleway's privacy terms are detailed and almost as strong as the best in this survey. By default it applies a zero-data-retention policy and collects only metadata and token counts. Two exceptions remain: prefix and KV caching (hashes and intermediate values, never prompt text) cannot be turned off, and requests that cause abnormal errors or look malicious may be stored in full "for up to two weeks." Data is not used for training. Scaleway acts as data processor and stores personal data in Paris. It states that "as a European company, Scaleway's AI services are not subject to extraterritorial laws such as the American Cloud Act" ([Scaleway data privacy](https://www.scaleway.com/en/docs/generative-apis/reference-content/data-privacy/)). Inference runs in a Paris data center operated by OPCORE ([Scaleway FAQ](https://github.com/scaleway/docs-content/blob/main/pages/generative-apis/faq.mdx)). Quotas apply once you register a payment method and rise after identity verification. Example headers show 600 requests and 1M tokens per minute, and you can set per-project limits and daily or monthly budgets ([Scaleway rate limits](https://github.com/scaleway/docs-content/blob/main/pages/generative-apis/reference-content/rate-limits.mdx)).

The other big clouds offer Qwen, gpt-oss and Llama 3.3 but nothing from the Kimi, DeepSeek or GLM tier, and two of them shut hobbyists out. **OVHcloud** has the cheapest gpt-oss-120b in Europe at €0.08/€0.40, plus Qwen3.8-27B at €0.40/€2.70 and Qwen3.5-397B at €0.60/€3.60 ([OVH catalog](https://www.ovhcloud.com/en/public-cloud/ai-endpoints/catalog/)). It allows anonymous trial calls at 2 requests per minute, and 400 per minute per model with a key. Its only privacy statement is a short one: "data is not stored or shared during or after model use" ([OVH capabilities](https://docs.ovhcloud.com/en/guides/public-cloud/ai-machine-learning/ai-endpoints-capabilities)). **IONOS** has the strongest written guarantee of any EU cloud. Prompts and outputs are never logged or persisted, there are no third-party sub-processors, only EU staff have operational access, and the DPA applies without a signature ([IONOS data handling](https://docs.ionos.com/cloud/ai/ai-model-hub/governance-and-compliance/data-handling)). But its price list says the offer is "for commercial use only" ([IONOS price list](https://docs.ionos.com/cloud/support/general-information/price-list/ionos-cloud-eur-en)). **T-Systems** serves GLM 5.2 and GLM 5.3 Flash from German data centers and is the only provider that documents streamed function calling ([T-Systems function calling](https://docs.llmhub.t-systems.net/guides/function-calling/)). Its entry plan, however, carries a **€1,000 monthly minimum** ([T-Systems Essential plan](https://docs.llmhub.t-systems.net/plans/essential/)). **STACKIT** checks trial registrations by hand. It keeps your email and subject ID in service logs for 30 days ([STACKIT service certificate](https://stackit.com/en/asset/download/61500/file/Service_Certificate_STACKIT_AI_Model_Serving_1-4.pdf?version=10)), and it warns that sending `"tools": []` can make Llama models invent tool calls ([STACKIT FAQ](https://docs.stackit.cloud/products/data-and-ai/ai-model-serving/faq/)). Swiss **Infomaniak** is the only established European cloud with any Kimi model, Kimi-K2.6 at CHF 0.60/3.00 ([Infomaniak prices](https://www.infomaniak.com/en/hosting/ai-tools/prices)). That model is a generation behind, and Moonshot stops offering K2 models to new users of its own API from 31 October ([Kimi platform docs](https://platform.kimi.ai/docs/models)).

## Kimi and MiniMax live at young Nordic, Irish and French specialists

Kimi K3, MiniMax M3.1 Flash and the 2.4T Qwen are found only at newer EU-native inference companies, and their privacy postures differ a lot. **Berget AI** (Berget AI AB, Sweden) publishes a machine-readable catalog. In it, `moonshotai/Kimi-K3` costs **€3/€15 (cached input €0.90)** with `function_calling`, `streaming` and `vision` all set to true, has an end-of-life date of 6 November, and is marked for upgrade to `moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro`. The catalog also lists GLM-5.3-Flash at €0.25/€0.50 (FP8, vision, tools) and Gemma 4 31B at €0.25/€0.50 ([Berget models API](https://api.berget.ai/v1/models)). Berget's DPA was updated on 22 October 2026 and is the most concrete privacy document in this research. Serverless inference "does not store prompts, uploaded or streamed files, or outputs after completed inference calls," and nothing is written to persistent disks. Logs hold only metadata (HTTP calls, status codes, timestamps, token counts, API keys). Nothing is used for training. The only sub-processors are three Swedish infrastructure firms and Stripe's Irish entity for payments ([Berget DPA](https://berget.ai/dpa)). The drawback is billing: "We don't offer pure pay-as-you-go without a plan." After a €5 trial, which needs a card, the Starter plan costs €25 per month, and unused credits stay valid for 12 months ([Berget pricing](https://berget.ai/pricing)).

**Nebius Token Factory** (Nebius Group N.V., Netherlands, NASDAQ-listed) has the widest cheap catalog. A third-party snapshot from 20 October lists Kimi K3 at $3/$15, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at $0.30/$1.20, GLM-5.3 at $1.40/$4.40, GLM-5.3-Flash at $0.15/$0.50, gpt-oss-120b at $0.15/$0.60, MiniMax M3 at $0.30/$1.20 and Muse Spark Open at $1.25/$4.25 ([BenchLM Nebius](https://benchlm.ai/hosts/nebius)). Its defaults, though, are the least private of any EU-native company. "Unless you enable Zero Data Retention, we keep your inputs and outputs to speed up inference." Serverless endpoints run in a location shown as "Global," decided dynamically. Nebius group entities in the US, Israel or Serbia may access data, though Nebius never trains on it ([Nebius legal quick guide](https://docs.tokenfactory.nebius.com/legal/legal-quick-guide)). Limits start at 60 RPM and 400k TPM and scale automatically up to a 20x ceiling ([Nebius rate limits](https://docs.tokenfactory.nebius.com/ai-models-inference/rate-limits.md)). If you use Nebius at all, turn on ZDR on day one.

The smaller specialists fill the gaps, with thinner documentation. **Sference** is headquartered in London according to PitchBook ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/1440493-12)) and says "every request runs on European GPUs." It lists Kimi K3 at $3/$15, GLM-5.3 at $1.20/$4.20 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at $0.50/$1.50, all with tools ([Sference models API](https://api.sference.com/v1/models)). It says it "never trains on customer data," and it offers a Flex tier at 0.7x price for requests that can wait up to 30 minutes, but it targets companies ([Sference](https://www.sference.com)). Irish **TensorX** advertises "TensorX-owned EU GPUs" and "zero data retention by design." It lists Kimi K3, GLM-5.3, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017, MiniMax M3.1 Flash and Qwen3.8-2.4T ([TensorX](https://tensorx.ai/)), but its data-center cities are reported inconsistently. Lyon-based **Arkane** processes payloads in France with zero retention ([Cortecs: Arkane](https://cortecs.ai/providerView/arkane)). Swedish **Inceptron** lists GLM-5.3 at 1.20/2.20 per million (currency not stated) and Kimi K2.6 and K2.7-Code, all with tools ([Inceptron models API](https://api.inceptron.io/v1/models)). German **Lyceum** claims Kimi K3, DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 at $2/$4 and Qwen3.8-2.4T at $2.50/$6.00, served from Paris and Finland, and says "we don't keep your prompts or outputs" ([Lyceum models](https://lyceum.technology/models/)). These claims are self-reported, and Lyceum's underlying operator is not disclosed. Italian **Regolo** (Seeweb) has a no-card 30-day trial and says retention is "Never," but it lacks Kimi and DeepSeek. Its gpt-oss-120b, at €1.00/€4.20, costs 6 to 12 times the market rate ([Regolo pricing](https://regolo.ai/pricing)).

**Mistral** now hosts other vendors' open models alongside its own Large 4, Medium 3.5 and Small 4: GLM 5.3, GLM 5.3 Flash and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 ([Mistral models](https://docs.mistral.ai/getting-started/models/)). That means you can use Chinese models through a French company. Its prices for these models were not visible, though, and its defaults need care. Free-tier API data "may" be used for training. Pay-as-you-go customers can opt out ([Mistral help](https://help.mistral.ai/en/articles/347617-do-you-use-my-user-data-to-train-your-artificial-intelligence-models)). Zero retention exists only on paid plans, on request, with a "legitimate reason" that Mistral can refuse ([Mistral ZDR](https://docs.mistral.ai/admin/monitor-comply/zero-data-retention)).

**Cortecs** (Cortecs GmbH, Vienna) is the one aggregator that fits your constraints. Requests "remain within Europe" by default. Its **Sovereign Cloud** setting limits routing to 12 EU-based providers (AKI, Arkane, Berget, Entrim, Inceptron, Infercom, IONOS, Mistral, Nebius, OVH, Scaleway, TensorX), and a ZDR setting routes only to non-retaining endpoints. Cortecs signs one DPA as the sole processor and treats the providers as its sub-processors ([Cortecs data governance](https://docs.cortecs.ai/security-and-compliance/data-governance-and-controls)). It bills in EUR from prepaid card top-ups with a 5% service fee; its homepage and pricing page disagree on whether the fee is charged on top-ups or on tokens ([Cortecs pricing](https://cortecs.ai/pricing)). It describes its rate limits as "unrestricted access to platform-wide limits" ([Cortecs](https://cortecs.ai)). Its live catalog of 108 models covers everything on the shortlist, including **MiniMax M3.1 Flash, available only through TensorX** ([Cortecs models API](https://api.cortecs.ai/v1/models)). Two caveats apply. Without Sovereign Cloud, the default pool also includes AWS, Google and Azure EU regions and UK-headquartered Sference, and some models (MiniMax M2.x, Qwen3-32B) route only through AWS Ireland. Nebius also stays in the Sovereign pool even though it retains data by default, so exclude Nebius explicitly.

## Hyperscalers, US hosts and vendor APIs each fail one test

Every option outside the EU-native group fails at least one of your requirements: EU processing, current models, default privacy, or a jurisdiction where you could enforce your rights.

| Route | Best case for you | Why it is not the primary |
|---|---|---|
| AWS Bedrock (EU regions) | OpenAI-compatible endpoint; ZDR via `data_retention_mode: none`; gpt-oss, Qwen3, DeepSeek V3.2, Kimi K2.5 and GLM 5 run in-region (Stockholm) | EU in-region models are a generation old; Kimi K3 and GLM 5.3 are Global-only; US company |

| US specialists | zero retention by default at most; lowest prices; fastest speeds | no self-serve EU serverless; Together stores prompts by default; Groq logs up to 30 days |

**AWS Bedrock** is the only hyperscaler that keeps a broad set of these models inside EU regions behind an OpenAI-compatible endpoint (`https://bedrock-runtime.<region>.amazonaws.com/openai/v1`) with documented tool calling and streaming ([Bedrock endpoints](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/endpoints.html)). The EU in-region models are older, though. Stockholm has DeepSeek V3.2, Kimi K2.5 and GLM 5, while **Kimi K3 and GLM 5.3 are reachable from EU regions only through Global cross-region inference** ([Bedrock regional availability](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/models-region-compatibility.html)). Stockholm prices run about 20% above US rates, for example DeepSeek V3.2 at $0.74/$2.22 ([Bedrock pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/pricing/)). Guaranteed zero retention requires setting `data_retention_mode` to `none` ([Bedrock data retention](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/data-retention.html)). **Azure's** EU Data Zone covers only DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Mistral-Large-3 among these models, and every Kimi deployment is Global-only ([Azure region availability](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/foundry-models/concepts/models-sold-directly-by-azure-region-availability)). **Vertex AI** makes no EU ML-processing commitment for any DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi, GLM, gpt-oss or MiniMax model ([Vertex data residency](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/learn/data-residency)). All of these are US companies, and AWS and Microsoft stay subject to the CLOUD Act even in EU data centers ([Kontinent](https://kontinent.ai/en/blog/gpu-prices-europe)).

The US specialists have good privacy defaults but no self-serve EU processing. **Fireworks** keeps no data for open models by default, but its default serverless routing means "Any model | Any region," and EU-only serverless is available only through sales ([Fireworks data handling](https://docs.fireworks.ai/guides/security_compliance/data_handling); [Fireworks data residency](https://docs.fireworks.ai/accounts/data-residency)). **DeepInfra** keeps inputs only in memory, logs no content ([DeepInfra data](https://deepinfra.com/docs/data)), and is the cheapest host for nearly every model, but it documents no EU region. **Cerebras** keeps no inputs or outputs ([Cerebras privacy](https://www.cerebras.ai/privacy-policy)) and runs gpt-oss-120b at about 1,663 tokens/s ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gpt-oss-120b/providers)). **Together AI** is the exception: it "stores the prompts you send and the responses models return, and may use them for product improvements" unless an admin turns this off ([Together privacy](https://docs.together.ai/docs/privacy-and-security)). Novita already lists `kimi-k3.1-pro` at $2/$10 ([Novita models API](https://api.novita.ai/openai/v1/models)). Since no weights are public yet, it is probably passing requests through to Moonshot.

Of the vendors' own APIs, **DeepSeek's is the clearest no**. It "collect[s], process[es] and store[s] your Personal Data in People's Republic of China" and lists training as a purpose. The only opt-out is an email ([DeepSeek privacy policy](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-privacy-policy.html)). Its API terms fall under mainland Chinese law and include no DPA ([DeepSeek Open Platform ToS](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-open-platform-terms-of-service.html)). Berlin's data protection authority reported the app to Apple and Google as illegal content under the DSA because of unlawful transfers to China ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/27/germany-tells-apple-google-to-remove-deepseek-from-the-countrys-app-stores)). Both refused to remove it, and as of June 2026 the authority was escalating ([iphone-ticker.de](https://www.iphone-ticker.de/apple-und-google-verweigern-deepseek-sperre-280220/)). **Moonshot's** July 2026 API terms, with a Singapore contracting entity, say "we may use Content to provide, maintain, develop, support, and improve the Services." The only way to restrict that is an enterprise agreement ([Kimi model use agreement](https://platform.kimi.ai/docs/agreement/modeluse)). **Z.ai** looks best on paper: its API DPA says it does "not store any of the content." It still names no GDPR representative and no transfer mechanism ([Z.ai privacy policy](https://docs.z.ai/legal-agreement/privacy-policy)). MiniMax's official API privacy page could not be read. A third-party tracker says data is stored in the US and may be transferred to the Shanghai parent ([StandardCompute](https://standardcompute.com/providers/minimax)). Because EU hosts serve the same open weights, none of these APIs is needed except Moonshot's, and only for the week before K3.1 Pro weights ship.

**OpenRouter** can enforce `zdr: true` and `data_collection: "deny"` per account or per request on its free Standard plan ([OpenRouter provider routing](https://openrouter.ai/docs/features/provider-routing)). True EU-only processing needs the Business plan, which adds an 8% fee, and the `eu.openrouter.ai` endpoint. Even then, account and abuse metadata "may be processed in the US," and the EU catalog has no Kimi K3, no DeepSeek V4 Pro and no Qwen ([OpenRouter Business EU](https://openrouter.ai/business/eu)). A policy update dated 27 October also screens all requests, ZDR included, for CSAM, and flagged content may be kept ([OpenRouter ZDR](https://openrouter.ai/docs/features/zdr)). For you, it offers nothing that Cortecs does not, and it adds a US company to the path. **Requesty** runs a Frankfurt gateway with an org-level EU restriction ([Requesty EU routing](https://docs.requesty.ai/features/eu-routing)), but its pages disagree on whether zero retention is the default ([Requesty security](https://www.requesty.ai/security)).

## ChatOpenAI drops the reasoning Kimi needs between tool calls

Any of the endpoints above works with the app's existing `OPENAI_BASE_URL` path. The trouble starts after the first tool call. The app builds a plain `langchain_openai.ChatOpenAI`, calls `bind_tools`, and runs a LangGraph loop (`backend/services/agent.py`). That loop sends the whole message history, including the previous assistant turn with its `tool_calls`, back to the model after every tool runs. Kimi K3 was trained in "preserved thinking" mode. For multi-turn and tool-call conversations, the full assistant message, **including `reasoning_content` and `tool_calls`, must be passed back unchanged** ([GitHub MoonshotAI/Kimi-K3](https://github.com/MoonshotAI/Kimi-K3)). The installed langchain-openai 1.1.12 says directly in its source that "non-standard response fields added by third-party providers (e.g., `reasoning_content`, `reasoning_details`) are **not** extracted or preserved." When it turns an assistant message back into a request, it writes `content`, `tool_calls` and `name`, but no reasoning field ([langchain-openai source](https://github.com/langchain-ai/langchain/blob/master/libs/partners/openai/langchain_openai/chat_models/base.py)). So from the second model call of every tool chain, Kimi sees its own earlier turn without its reasoning. Nobody has tested whether that produces HTTP 400 errors or just worse answers. Either way, a naive comparison would underrate Kimi. The fix is a small `ChatOpenAI` subclass that copies `reasoning_content` from responses and stream deltas into `additional_kwargs`, then writes it back into outgoing assistant messages. The same subclass would let the app's reasoning-steps UI show the model's thinking, which today is thrown away. Qwen3.8 with `preserve_thinking` raises the same issue ([GitHub QwenLM/Qwen3.8](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.8)).

Reasoning effort needs per-model handling too. The app adds `Reasoning: medium` to the start of the system prompt whenever the provider is "openai," and every custom base URL counts as "openai" (`backend/services/llm_provider.py`). That line is gpt-oss's convention and does nothing for the other models. Kimi sets effort through `reasoning_effort` (low, high or max), and **the default is max** ([GitHub MoonshotAI/Kimi-K3](https://github.com/MoonshotAI/Kimi-K3)). With thinking tokens billed as output at €15 per million, the default is the biggest cost driver in Kimi tests. On Bedrock, Kimi K3 output tokens even count tenfold against quotas ([Bedrock Kimi K3 card](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-card-moonshot-ai-kimi-k3.html)). `ChatOpenAI` has a `reasoning_effort` string field, but its docs list only OpenAI's values (minimal to high). Values like `max` or Scaleway's `none` need testing, and so does the app's default `temperature=0` with models that always think.

Smaller traps follow from the provider notes. Scaleway's gpt-oss-120b needs the Responses API for tools (`use_responses_api=True`), or use a different host. Cortecs can switch upstream providers between requests, so tool-call formatting can vary unless you pin providers ([Cortecs models API](https://api.cortecs.ai/v1/models)). Only T-Systems explicitly documents streamed tool-call deltas, so every other host needs a `stream=True` test with tools bound. GLM-5.3 accepts text only on Scaleway, Mistral and Bedrock. For vision, the app's separate `VISION_MODEL` and `VISION_BASE_URL` settings (`backend/config.py`) can point at a vision-capable Qwen3.8-27B or DeepSeek V4.1 Flash. The same file defaults `WHISPER_BASE_URL` and `TTS_BASE_URL` to `OPENAI_BASE_URL`. Switching the base URL to an EU inference host therefore also redirects voice transcription and speech requests, which those hosts may not support. Either set `USE_LOCAL_WHISPER=true` or point those two variables somewhere else.

## A 24 GB Radeon runs the 27-35B tier; frontier models need a rented rack

At home, memory decides what you can run. A Q4 quantization takes about 0.55 to 0.6 bytes per parameter plus KV cache. By that rule, a single 24 GB card such as an RX 7900 XTX fits Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (fast, with only 3B parameters active), Qwen3.8-27B, Gemma 4 31B or 26B-A3.8B, and gpt-oss-20b, which needs about 16 GB ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-oss/)). Gemma 4 lists AMD ROCm among its supported runtimes, along with Ollama, llama.cpp and vLLM ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemma-4/)). A 32 GB Radeon AI PRO R9700, or two cards, adds longer context and gpt-oss-120b with its experts offloaded to system RAM. A 128 GB Strix Halo (Ryzen AI Max) reaches into the 100-300B MoE range. GLM-5.3-Flash's 1-bit GGUF is 93 GB, with an estimated 10-20 tok/s on such a machine. The same guide recommends Qwen3.6-35B-A3B locally on 24 GB GPUs, with an API for hard problems ([RunAIHome](https://runaihome.com/blog/glm-5-3-open-weights-live-hardware-guide-2026/)). The frontier is out of reach: Kimi K3's weights are about **1.4 TB** ([Techi](https://www.techi.com/kimi-k3-open-weights-inference-economics/)), and GLM-5.3 needs about 450 GB at 4-bit and 750 GB at FP8 ([RunAIHome](https://runaihome.com/blog/glm-5-3-open-weights-live-hardware-guide-2026/)).

For server software, AMD now ships an official vLLM 0.29.0 image on ROCm 10.1.0. It lists RX 9070/9060, RX 7900/7800/7700/7600, Radeon PRO W7900-class cards, Radeon AI PRO R9700 and Ryzen AI Max (gfx1151) as supported, and notes a slow-warmup bug on Radeon in vLLM 0.21-0.25 ([AMD ROCm docs](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/projects/ai-ecosystem/en/latest/inference/vllm.html)). Community reports still call consumer RDNA support "experimental" ([lemonade-sdk](https://github.com/lemonade-sdk/vllm-rocm/)), and an RDNA4 FP8 patch has not been merged upstream ([vLLM issue #28649](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/28649)). In one RX 9070 XT benchmark, llama.cpp's `llama-server` on Vulkan reached 62 tok/s against vLLM ROCm's 48 ([llama-server-rdna4-vulkan](https://github.com/ivangotoy/llama-server-rdna4-vulkan)). It also exposes an OpenAI-compatible API with Jinja-template tool calling ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/rosgluk/llamacpp-vs-ollama-in-2026-which-runtime-should-you-run-4k7f)). Ollama is the easiest to set up, but it lags on new models: Gemma 4's tool-call and streaming bugs in Ollama v0.20.0 were fixed in llama.cpp first ([gist](https://gist.github.com/daniel-farina/87dc1c394b94e45bb700d27e9ea03193)). The practical choice is `llama-server --jinja` as the default (try Vulkan on RDNA4), and AMD's `rocm/vllm` image when a model fits in VRAM and you want vLLM's tool parsers, such as `qwen3_coder` and `minimax_m3`. Either one plugs straight into `OPENAI_BASE_URL`.

Renting EU GPUs to run frontier weights privately is possible but makes no sense for hobby testing. H100s start at €2.80 per GPU-hour at OVHcloud, IONOS rents H200s at €3.00 billed per minute, and Scaleway's 8x B300 nodes cost €7.50 per GPU-hour ([Kontinent](https://kontinent.ai/en/blog/gpu-prices-europe)). An 8x B300 node large enough for Kimi K3 therefore costs about €60 per hour. That is the price of 4M Kimi output tokens at Berget, before counting setup time.

## Conclusion

In 2026, an EU location is necessary but not enough. Privacy now depends mostly on each account's defaults. An EU-native company (Nebius) keeps prompts unless you turn ZDR on, the EU's own model vendor (Mistral) reserves the right to train on free-tier data, Bedrock needs `data_retention_mode: none`, and even Scaleway keeps error payloads for two weeks. Meanwhile, the price gap between EU hosting and Chinese first-party APIs comes to a few euros per hobby month. So the useful habit is to choose the jurisdiction once and then check every account's retention and training settings, rather than shopping on price.

The bigger change is that "OpenAI-compatible" no longer guarantees compatibility. Every top open model is now a thinking model, and Kimi and Qwen expect their reasoning to come back on the next call. That makes the app's single generic `ChatOpenAI` the weakest link, more than any provider. A thin provider adapter would make every future model test meaningful. It would capture and resend reasoning, map effort settings per model, and treat model IDs as configuration refreshed from `/v1/models`, because EU catalogs now change weekly. Without it, results reflect integration bugs as much as model quality.
